1 Suggested Hierarchy (Who Does What)

A lean, credible structure. Editorial decisions must stay fully independent of money — this is the single most important governance rule in academic publishing.

Key hiring truths for a slow start

  • You can wear many hats initially (publisher + managing editor + tech admin). Add paid/free roles as submissions grow.
  • The EIC should be a senior academic (professor/PharmD/PhD with publications) — not a student. ISSN India explicitly rejects boards with students/research fellows, and requires institutional e-mails for board members.
  • International board members (5–10) dramatically improve credibility, reviewer reach and Scopus/DOAJ chances later.

4 Step-by-Step Plan (Online-Only Journal)

The complete walk-through. Costs in ₹ (INR) unless marked; all registrations below are for an online-only journal.

Step 0 — Decide these 5 things first (Week 1)

  1. Niche: e.g., Journal of Clinical Pharmacy & Therapeutics, Pharmacovigilance & Drug Safety, Pharmaceutical Nanotechnology, Pharmacy Practice & Education. Pick ONE — niche journals get indexed faster.
  2. Name: unique (search the ISSN portal and Google), easy to remember, .org domain available.
  3. Business model: Diamond OA (free for authors & readers — best for credibility, funded by you/institution) or APC-based OA (₹5,000–15,000 typical for Indian journals, with waivers). Avoid subscription-only for a new journal.
  4. Frequency: start quarterly (4 issues/year) — ISSN rules and reviewer capacity are happiest with a steady, honest cadence.
  5. Entity: sole proprietorship (simplest), LLP (recommended — limited liability, ~₹5–15k all-in), or run it under your college/university if you're faculty (fastest route, but the institution owns it).

Step 1 — Legal setup (Months 1–2)

  • Register your entity (MCA portal for LLP/Pvt Ltd; or just PAN + Shops & Establishment for sole prop).
  • Open a current account in the journal's name; keep all journal money separate.
  • MSME/Udyam registration — free, 10 minutes, useful for startup benefits.
  • GST — register only when turnover crosses ₹20 lakh (services); until then you don't need it.
  • Trademark your journal name + logo (IP India portal, ₹4,500/class e-filing for individuals/startups/SMEs) — do it before you publish, not after.
  • Draft board appointment letters, reviewer agreements, and a privacy policy (DPDP Act 2023 covers author/reader data you collect).

Step 2 — ISSN (Months 2–4) ⭐ your key registration

  • Online-only journals do NOT need Press Registrar (RNI/PRGI) registration. Go straight to ISSN.
  • Apply online at the ISSN National Centre India — National Science Library, CSIR-NIScPR, New Delhi (nsl.niscpr.res.in → ISSN Process → Apply Online). Free of cost.
  • Before applying, your website must be live with: aims & scope, frequency, starting year, publisher name + full address, author guidelines, submission info, and an Editorial Board page (senior faculty only, institutional emails mandatory).
  • Expect: 3 random board members get verification emails and must reply within 5 days or the application is rejected.
  • Your ISSN is provisional until your 2nd issue is published and you inform NSL — then it's confirmed and appears on the international ISSN portal (portal.issn.org).
  • Want print too? First register the title with the Press Registrar General of India on the Press Sewa portal (PRP Act 2023, fully online), then apply for the print ISSN separately.

Step 3 — Platform (Months 2–4, parallel with Step 2)

  • Buy domain (~₹800–1,500/yr) + hosting with SSL (~₹10–25k/yr).
  • Install OJS (Open Journal Systems) — free, open source, used by thousands of journals; handles submission, blind review, publishing, DOI deposit. Self-host on cPanel if you have tech help; otherwise buy managed OJS hosting (easier, ~US$400–800/yr).
  • Create essential pages: About, Aims & Scope, Editorial Board, Author Guidelines, Peer Review Process, Publication Ethics, APC policy, Contact, Archives.
  • Set up: journal email (editorial@yourjournal.org), ORCID integration, Google Analytics/Search Console, LinkedIn/X/WhatsApp channel.

Step 4 — Editorial governance (Months 3–5)

  • Recruit an Editor-in-Chief — a respected professor with a publication record. If you're early-career, ask a senior mentor to be EIC while you're Managing Editor.
  • Build the Editorial Board (25–40): senior faculty (Professor/Associate Professor level, PharmD/PhD) across pharma specialties; 5–10 international names; all with institutional emails.
  • Build a reviewer pool of 50–100: approach authors of recent papers in your niche, board members' colleagues, conference speakers.
  • Adopt policies (align with COPE + ICMJE): ethics/malpractice, plagiarism (≤10–15% similarity per UGC norms), conflict of interest, authorship criteria, data availability, AI-use policy, corrections/retractions.
  • Define the workflow in OJS: submission → desk screening (48–72 hrs) → 2 external reviewers (double-blind) → decision (accept/minor/major/reject) → revision → copyedit → proof → publish. Target: first decision ≤6 weeks.

Step 5 — Credibility infrastructure (Months 4–6)

  • Crossref membership: $275/yr (revenue under $1M tier; $200 if under $1,000) + $1.00 per article DOI. Get your DOI prefix, install the OJS Crossref plugin. This is the #1 trust signal — do it before your first issue.
  • Plagiarism: Crossref Similarity Check / iThenticate — first 100 checks free per year, then ~$0.75/doc. Start with this; don't rely on free tools alone.
  • Google Scholar: create the journal profile; ensure clean metadata so articles appear automatically.
  • COPE membership: £213/yr for journals with <12 issues/yr — you can apply after 12 months of publishing; meanwhile just follow COPE guidelines (free).
  • Set up long-term archiving (CLOCKSS/Portico via OJS plugin) and regular backups.

Step 6 — Soft launch: pilot issue (Months 6–8)

  • Invite 6–8 solid papers from researchers you know (no/low APC) for Volume 1, Issue 1.
  • Run the entire pipeline end-to-end; measure where it gets stuck (reviewer no-shows? copyedit? metadata?) and fix.
  • Publish with complete metadata: DOI on every article, ORCIDs, references deposited with Crossref, PDF + HTML (XML if you can).

Step 7 — Full launch (Months 9–12)

  • Open a formal Call for Papers; first regular quarterly issue.
  • Market: pharmacy colleges and PG/PhD guides (India has a huge M.Pharm/PharmD author base), LinkedIn, pharmacy conferences, WhatsApp/Telegram research groups, pharmacy societies.
  • Publish a transparent APC sheet (amount, waivers for students/low-income countries) — or Diamond OA.
  • After 12 months: join COPE; apply to DOAJ (free — needs strong policies + ~5–10 quality articles).

Step 8 — Year 2+: growth flywheel

  • Confirm ISSN with NSL after issue 2 (provisional → confirmed).
  • Apply to Scopus (~2 years of consistent quarterly publication, quality + international board required) and Web of Science ESCI; PubMed Central if your quality is exceptional (pharmacy journals often eligible via the international PMC track).
  • Annual compliance: GST/income-tax returns, trademark renewals, policy updates.
  • Grow: themed special issues, conference partnerships, institutional memberships.

8 Pitfalls That Kill New Journals (Avoid These)

  • Publishing without real peer review. A journal with 5 fake "accepted in 3 days" papers is instantly blacklisted by DOAJ/Scopus and the community. Slow is safe.
  • Students on the editorial board. ISSN India rejects these outright, and DOAJ/Scopus will too. Senior faculty with institutional emails only.
  • Buying indexing or "impact factor". No legitimate database sells inclusion. Anyone offering it is a scammer.
  • Chasing UGC-CARE. The CARE list was discontinued in early 2025 — don't pay anyone claiming to get you on it.
  • No AI-use policy. In 2025–26 reviewers and databases expect explicit AI-writing and AI-review rules. Write one before the first submission.
  • Unclear APC policy. Hidden or changing fees destroy trust faster than anything. Publish your APC table and waiver policy on the homepage.
  • Skipping DOIs. A journal without DOIs looks amateur and won't be indexed properly. Crossref year one, no exceptions.
  • Inflated claims. Never claim Scopus/PubMed indexing you don't have. Accuracy on your site is a legal and reputational issue.
  • Ignoring archiving. If your server dies, 5 years of content dies. CLOCKSS/Portico + backups from day one.
