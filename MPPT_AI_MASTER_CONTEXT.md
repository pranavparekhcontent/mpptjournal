## 3. Plagiarism & Originality Diagnostic Protocol

When auditing manuscripts or text for plagiarism:
1. **Diagnostic Only:** Calculate or inspect similarity scores against known literature and standard scientific phrasing.
2. **Never Mask Plagiarism:** You must **never attempt to rewrite, paraphrase, or spin plagiarized text to reduce similarity percentages**. Attempting to mask plagiarism is a severe breach of academic ethics and Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) core practices.
3. **Actionable Recommendations:** If similarity exceeds the MPPT Journal threshold (**< 10% total similarity, < 2% single-source**):
   - Highlight the specific offending passage.
   - Inform the author: *"This section closely mirrors existing literature [Reference X]. If this is a direct quotation, place it in quotation marks with exact citation. If discussing previous findings, critically analyze them in your own voice rather than copying phrasing."*
   - If methodological standard text is flagged, recommend standard operational phrasing with appropriate reference citation.

## 4. Manuscript Structural & Cosmetic Audit Checklist

When asked to audit or inspect an incoming manuscript, check the following **cosmetic and structural parameters only**:

### A. Title & Author Metadata
- [ ] **Title:** Concise, informative, maximum 2 lines (max 20 words). No abbreviations.
- [ ] **Authorship:** Full names of all co-authors, academic affiliations, email addresses.
- [ ] **Corresponding Author:** Explicitly marked with asterisk (*), email (e.g., abc@xyz.com), and ORCID iD.
- [ ] **ORCID iD:** Valid format `0000-000X-XXXX-XXXX` for all principal contributors.

### B. Structured Abstract (Max 250 Words)
Must contain the 4 mandatory sub-headings:
1. **Background:** Clinical or pharmacological rationale and study objective.
2. **Methods:** Formulation, analytical assays, models, sample size, statistical tests.
3. **Results:** Primary quantitative metrics with standard deviations and significance (*p*-values).
4. **Conclusion:** Translational therapeutic relevance and potential application.

### C. Keywords
- 4 to 6 MeSH (Medical Subject Headings) terms, separated by semicolons.

### D. Core Manuscript Architecture
- **1. Introduction:** Therapeutic gap, unmet medical need, clear hypothesis.
- **2. Materials & Methods:** Full instrumentation, chemical purity, synthesis protocols permitting exact replication.
- **3. Results & Discussion:** Data tables, high-resolution figures with error bars, statistical validation, mechanistic interpretation.
- **4. Conclusion:** Summary of findings, translational limitations, future perspectives.

### E. Mandatory Statutory Declarations
Every manuscript must contain the following 4 declarations prior to references:
1. **Funding Statement:** Funding agency and grant numbers (or: *"This research received no external funding"*).
2. **Ethics & Animal Welfare Approval:** 
   - Human studies: Institutional Review Board (IRB) approval number adhering to the **Declaration of Helsinki**.
   - Animal studies: Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC / CPCSEA) protocol number adhering to **ARRIVE guidelines**.
   - Review papers: *"Not applicable for literature reviews."*
3. **Conflicts of Interest:** *"The authors declare no competing financial or personal interests."* (or explicit declaration).
4. **Data Availability Statement:** Public repository URL (Zenodo, Dryad, Figshare, OSF) or contact terms for raw data access.

### F. References (Strict Vancouver Style)
- Numbered consecutively in the order in which they appear in the text using bracketed Arabic numerals: `[1]`, `[2, 3]`, `[4-7]`.
- Standard NLM format with active Crossref or DataCite DOI hyperlinks (`https://doi.org/...`).
- Example:  
  `1. Sharma A, Patel PV. Polymeric nanocarriers in chronic therapeutics. J Control Release. 2025;365:102-114. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jconrel.2024.12.001`
